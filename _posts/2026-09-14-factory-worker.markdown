_Welcome to "That's my (User) Story and I'm sticking to it!" Tech tales for the curious and short of attention span._

You've probably heard this one before: the one where the engineer spots an inefficiency in production, fixes it, and saves the company millions.  

What's that engineer's title?  

Senior Application Engineer?  
Chief Technology Officer?  
Master Enterprise Cloud Architect Extraordinaire?  

How about _factory line worker?_

Let me tell you a quick story about Brandon Schenz.  

It was the 90's, and Brandon was in his early 20's. He was going through a rough patch, trying to get back on his feet after his first marriage fell apart. He was living in Columbus, Ohio working as a glass cleaner in a factory for Schwartz Glass.  

Cleaning glass at an industrial scale was more involved than spraying it with Windex; Brandon worked with industrial machinery in dangerous conditions handling thin, sharp glass _without_ gloves (at least, that's what Brandon told me. Seems like maybe it was a personal decision to _not_ wear gloves, so maybe Brandon was at fault for _some_ of his danger). He & his partner would prepare the glass by stacking the glass from largest to smallest _(like those ring tower toys for toddlers)_.  

The challenge was that the work orders were *NOT* in the same sort-order as the glass; so the factory workers spent _a lot_ of time working out all of that math each day.  

If there was a handy software engineer around, maybe _(s)he_ could figure out how to use a computer to do all of that math and pre-sort the work orders in the blink of an eye! But, like I said, this was the 90s. Software solutions aren't around every corner and under every rock just yet. Plus, Schwartz is a somewhat small operation, and _not_ on the cutting edge of technology.

Enter **Master Enterprise Cloud Architect Extraordinaire** Brandon Schenz.  

Using nothing but a piece of gum, a paper clip, and a free VB3 development environment he got from his college's student bookstore, Brandon wrote a program that sorted the work orders _in stacking order_. It took some tinkering. His partner agreed to help him out by testing his work each day. 

But when it was finished, the completed version of phase one of Brandon's program, did in fact, result in a **3X** productivity improvement! Brandon continued to iterate on his program at Schwartz until he eventually left the company. (Funny enough, his next role was "sales associate" with a tennis racket company.... Where, as you can imagine, he went outside his lane to deliver _more_ software. I guess his calling wasn't obvious to him, yet!)

What drove Brandon to do this? Not everyone would go the extra mile like this. 

It helped that he was already in school for programming, I suppose.  
And he might have been inspired by his partner's dad; who installed the nation's very first computerized telephone switching machine. _(Which, if you don't know what a telephone switching system is, then think of Rosemary from Hong Kong Phooie... And if you don't know what Hong Kong Phooie is, then nevermind)_.  
But I think Brandon just has that _stuff_ inside him that makes him that makes him want to see things work _better._  

By the way, in case you were wondering, he _is_ happily re-married to this day (sorry, ladies).
