# Drive

_Editor's note: In this piece, I have an apparent disdain for my peers and wife that make me almost seem like a psychopath. The reader should understand that I'm evaluating myself as though I'm someone else whom I'm extremely skeptical of; someone who **is** a self-absorbed psychopath. I spend a lot of time in this article trying to convince the psychopath to try to do the right thing despite their self-absorption._

**There is only one reason to work: to live.**

I demonstrated my belief in that from 2020-2022 when I quit my job.  

Back then, I lived alone and I didn't need a 6 figure income.  

Now, I work to support my family.  

But, as long as I have to work anyway, I find myself getting more interested and more passionate than reasonable.  

When anyone asked my Dad how his work was, he would say "Work was work".  

But when anyone asks _me_ how work was, I have to hold back a monologue.  

I hold back a monologue about what we're doing, why it's silly, why they should listen to me more, etc...  

I get frustrated at work. I carry the stress with me at home. It comes out at my family.  

I want to be like my Dad: I want work to be kept in it's place. I want to leave and forget about it. So why do I keep letting it get under my skin?  

**I have to know: Is there a good & noble reason for it?**

Some of my co-workers (the ones who I confide in) tell me it's because I'm "just that committed to quality."  

I think they might be willing to flatter me a bit. To them, it probably _feels_ nice to them to _say_ something nice. To say the easy thing.  

However, I think an _equally_ plausible explanation for the reason I get sucked into work is that it is a source of my ego.  

I want to be seen as the smartest guy. I want people to see that my ideas are correct. It doesn't even matter what they're about; the fact that it happens in the context of [whatever my work is] is incidental.  

So, faced with these two alternative theories about why I find work frustrating: which makes more sense? If I think about the other aspects of my life, which explanation is a better fit?  

- In my life outside of work, I am _not_ a detail-oriented person. On home DIY projects, I _definitely_ have a "good enough" mentality.  
- When I'm talking to my wife about _anything_, I definitely get frustrated when she doesn't understand what I mean or isn't as quick as me.  

Unfortunately, just looking at these two very very true and recurring aspects of my life, I'd say that the ego-problem theory looks more likely that the quality-commitment theory...  

.... But I find that hard to accept.......  

....... But I _can_ take two opposing ideas and hold them both in tension.......  

.... So, keeping in mind that it's still possible that I have an ego problem, let's just say that I _don't_. Let's take a charitable view of my plight. Let's say my diagnosis of the situation is correct. Let's say that I _do_ work with people who don't care as much as I do. And let's agre that that _is_ frustrating.  

In other words: Let's say "I'm the _adult_".  

Then, let's consider _all_ aspects of being the adult.  

**On being an adult: Well then it's about time I paid my dues** 

I've been the child for a long, long time. It's my turn to deal with clowns, since I was a clown for so long.  

**On being an adult: The hardest thing about being the adult is that no one is there to tell you and affirm you for it.** 

It's an entirely internal decision, perhaps between you & God, that you're going to keep tolerating/forgiving/compensating for the weaker party.  
No one will ever know that you did it.  
But you've got to let it go.  
And, no one can ever explicitly tell you that you did the right thing and reward you for it.  
Hell, perhaps you're not always _sure_ that it _is_ the right thing! But you have to have a conviction about it that outlasts your feelings.  

**On being an adult: but I _want_ to see work as an "a-moral" place.** 

A place where right-and-wrong do not apply, because it's strictly business and we're all there for the money...

Maybe it's time for me to incorporate a personal decision to do the "right thing" for the sake of keeping the peace.  
To accept their weaknesses, even their foolishness and their flaws, and try try _try_ to lead by example...  
... and to _still_ be less esteemed than they are by our mutual peers (because the institutions are _also_ flawed and they promote the wrong people)...  
... and to never expect anything in return...  
... (in this lifetime).

**... anyways...**

So if we take the most charitable view of my position; that _I'm_ smart and _my_ ideas are right and _I'm_ the hardest working one; that doesn't, on it's own, give me an excuse to leave. (Besides, if I do, I'm sure I'll continue to have these same complaints elsewhere).  

By staying, I _still_ have the opportunity to grow in:  

- Patience.
- Fatherhood.
- Technical skill.
- Maturity.
- A willingness to sacrifice for those who I think don't deserve it in a context that I think is beneath me.
- The acceptance of mystery and the unknown (who knows how I may grow; perhaps if I knew, then it wouldn't be growth).

_... None of this is even to say that the most charitable view _is_ the correct view, but it helps me to know that the conclusion is the same regardless._

# Conclusion: 

So, whether I have an ego problem or whether I'm the only adult on the team, the conclusion is:  

**There is only one reason to work: to live....**  ..... And the aim is to live **peacefully**, which up to on me.

# Epilogue

I'll know I'm on the right track if:

- I'm there when I'm needed.
- My teammates feel safe asking me for help.
- My team is disciplined _enough_ to get work done.
- Things are funny.
- I spend less time talking to people about other people and caring whether other people talk about me.
- I'm more "in the moment" with my family.
- I take the fall.

Things to _not_ do:

- Showing someone the error of their ways by example.
- Exposing someone for their lack of discipline.
- Make resume-driven decisions.

Red Herrings (things that might feel right but _aren't_):

- Getting my way on any given issue.
- Winning arguments head-to-head.
- Getting someone to agree with me about something in a back-alley conversation.
- Getting people to be on my side in inactionable ways that only feed & grow frustration.
- Giving "honest" feedback that does not leave room for redemption.
- Accomplishing something unilaterally.

Things to avoid: 

- The pit in my stomach when I know I've had to even _slightly_ modify my account of a story to stengthen my position.
- Being unable to sypmathize with the person I'm talking to.
- Trying to win an argument rather than find a solution.
